GeoSight: reading land cover from space

Draw an area on a map (up to ~150 km²) or upload a GeoTIFF. GeoSight pulls a cloud-filtered Sentinel-2 composite, labels every 10 m pixel and extracts the road network as a graph. I led the team and owned research and model training.

View code

Data

69,614 Sentinel-2 tiles using six bands (B2, B3, B4, B8, B11, B12) across Uttarakhand, Haryana, Sikkim, Delhi and Kanpur. With no hand-labelled masks available, labels were generated semi-automatically from NDVI, NDWI and NDBI, with thresholds tuned separately for each region.

Model

  1. ExperimentsStarted with LinkNet (ResNet-50 encoder), then moved to a stronger architecture for six-band input.
  2. Deployed land-cover modelU-Net++ with an EfficientNet-B4 encoder, 6 input bands, 4 classes (background, rural, urban, water). Dice + Focal loss, AdamW, mixed precision, flips/rotations/affine augmentation.
  3. Road modelA separate U-Net (ResNet-50) on RGB detects roads.
  4. Checkpoint selectionWithout ground truth, 28 saved epochs were compared on class-coverage quality across regions to choose the deployed checkpoint.

From pixels to a road graph

Urban masks are turned into a graph you can route on: morphological cleaning, a Frangi ridge filter with scales derived from real road widths (20 / 35 / 55 m at 10 m per pixel), skeletonisation, conversion to a networkx graph, and pruning of dead-end stubs shorter than 150 m. Each tile then passes a quality gate on connectivity (≥ 0.70), road density (≥ 500 m/km²) and dead-end ratio (≤ 0.35).

The app

React + Leaflet front end with a Flask API backed by PyTorch and Google Earth Engine: fetch and classify an area, detect roads, classify an uploaded GeoTIFF and download the results. It shows class percentages, confidence and a four-panel view (image, raw prediction, filtered prediction, overlay).
