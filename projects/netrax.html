NetraX: quantum-assisted eye screening

A screening tool for rural primary health centres: an operator uploads fundus photos, NetraX checks image quality, grades diabetic retinopathy on a 0–4 scale, explains the decision with Grad-CAM and routes referrals to an ophthalmologist. My part: the hybrid quantum-classical classifier.

My classifier

  1. FeaturesA fine-tuned DINOv2-base backbone at 518 px; the CLS token and mean patch token are joined into a 1,536-value vector. The backbone is shared with the classical classifier, so only one model sits in memory.
  2. CompressPCA down to 4 components, then standardised, one value per qubit.
  3. Quantum circuitPennyLane, 4 qubits: angle embedding → 4 basic entangler layers → Pauli-Z expectation values, wrapped as a PyTorch layer.
  4. Classify & ensembleA linear head maps 4 outputs to 5 grades. The final grade comes from whichever model (quantum or DINOv2) is more confident, and disagreements are flagged for review.

The rest of the system (team)

  • Quality checks on sharpness (Laplacian variance), illumination, contrast and resolution before grading.
  • Grad-CAM on DINOv2 patch tokens, mapped back through the resize and centre-crop, with percentile scaling so a few hot patches don't wash out the map.
  • Clinical workflow: four user roles, reviewer and ophthalmologist queues, a two-page PDF report, Hindi and English UI, and an offline-capable PWA.
